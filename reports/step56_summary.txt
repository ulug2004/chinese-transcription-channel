4.  What the control set can now answer, and 匈奴
======================================================================
   Of 93 two-character transcriptions:
     of a 2-syllable source             93  100.0%

   THE 匈奴 QUESTION. The supplement reads 匈奴 as Hung and discards
   奴 nɑ as a Chinese pejorative, which requires two characters to
   write a one-syllable word. In the control set that is 0 of 93,
   0.0%.

   AND 奴 ITSELF IS A SOUND CHARACTER IN THIS CORPUS. It occurs 7
   times in the verified pairs and all 7 are phonetic, writing the
   nu of manuṣya: 末奴沙, 摩奴闍, 摩奴娑, 摩奴曬. It never appends a
   category. The characters that do append one are 天 27, 鬼 9,
   樹 9, 國 8, 山 7, 城 2, and 奴 is not among them.

   WHAT THAT DOES AND DOES NOT SETTLE. It does not show that 奴 is
   part of the Xiongnu name. The case for dropping it rests on
   Chinese usage in ethnonyms, where 奴 'slave' is a known pejorative,
   and that is an argument from Chinese practice which this corpus
   cannot test. What it does show is that the supplement's stated
   reason, that 奴 is appended rather than read, is not supported by
   transcription practice and is contradicted by the only direct
   evidence held here. The entry has to make the Chinese-usage
   argument or drop the claim.
