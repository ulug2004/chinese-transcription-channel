1.  The disputed substitution, measured
====================================================================
The supplement says a source voiceless affricate is reachable through
the Chinese dź- because voicing is leaky. The corpus says:

   source c      -> dź      0 of 48      0.0%
   source c      -> tś     32 of 48     66.7%
   source c      -> t       8 of 48     16.7%
   source c, ch  -> dź      0 of 54      0.0%
   source j, jh  -> dź     13 of 33     39.4%
   source k      -> k     190 of 220    86.4%
   source k      -> g      12 of 220     5.5%
   source g, gh  -> g      52 of 90     57.8%
   source g, gh  -> k      16 of 90     17.8%
   source k      -> dź      1 of 220     0.5%
   source g, gh  -> dź      0 of 90      0.0%

   VERDICT: a source voiceless affricate written with dź- does not
   occur in this corpus, 48 opportunities. The supplement's reason
   for 臣 -> çin is not supported. A source VOICED affricate is a
   different matter: 13 of 33, 39.4%.

   These reproduce the paper exactly: source c is 48 opportunities,
   0 written with dz- and 8 with t- at 16.7%, which is what the
   supplement quotes for the other title.

   THIS COLUMN IS AN ORTHOGRAPHIC ILLUSION. The Codex Cumanicus is
   written in medieval Latin spelling, in which j stands for Turkic
   y- and c before a back vowel stands for k-. So jain, jol and jil
   are yay, yol and yil, and can, cun and coun are kan, kun and
   kavun. None of them is an affricate. What is left is the genuine
   voiceless affricate in c-, which is 0 of 48, and Old Turkic has
   no initial voiced affricate for the other route. So no word in
   these lexicons reaches the character on this reading.
