4.  Which suffix existed, counted in the lexicons
======================================================================
   A cheap transcription of a word that cannot be formed is worth
   nothing. These are headwords in the DLT index, the Codex and the
   Irk Bitig glossary, 9379 in all.

   -gAn  front  -gen/-ken      67 headwords, 33 glossed as habitual
        bezetgen       daima bezeten
        bitilgen       daima yazılan
        edergen        çok arıyan; hakkını arayan
        egilgen        daima eğilen, eğilebllen
   -gAn  back   -gan/-kan     144 headwords, 76 glossed as habitual
        agıtgan        daima çıkartan, yükselten
        anutgan        daima hazırlıklı, hazırlayan
        arıtgan        her zaman temizleyen, ayıklayan
        asurgan        çok aksıran
   -gUn  front  -gün/-kün      16 headwords, 0 glossed as habitual
   -gUn  back   -gun/-kun      26 headwords, 0 glossed as habitual
   -gIn  front  -gin/-kin      18 headwords, 0 glossed as habitual
   -gIn  back   -gın/-kın      12 headwords, 0 glossed as habitual

   The -gAn rows carry the habitual glosses: daima, her zaman, cok.
   The -gUn rows are concrete nouns, birds and plants and body parts.
   In the eleventh-century record -gAn is the productive participle
   and -gUn is not. The modern durgun, coşkun, ötgün belong to a
   later productivity of -gUn and are not evidence for this period.

   THE HARMONY TEST. -gAn has two shapes, front and back, so a front
   stem takes -gen whatever its rounding. -gIn and -gUn have four, so
   a front ROUNDED stem such as kön- must take -gün and cannot take
   -gin. That removes köngin, which the transcription prices as
   cheaply as köngen, on grounds that have nothing to do with Chinese.

   THE STEM. kön- is attested as a verb in the same dictionary:
        köndgermek     doğrultmak, düzeltmek, dikmek yola kılavuzlama
        köndgürmek     doğrultmak
        köndgürtmek    dogrultmak, diktirmek
        könikmek       arkadaşlarından geri kalacak derecede zayıflık
        könitmek       dogrultmak,II, 313
        könmek         düzelmek, doğrulmak; yola gelmek;inkârdan sonr

5.  Where that leaves the name
======================================================================
   köngen, the participle of an attested verb meaning to become
   straight or upright, costs 19.4%, about 1 in 5.
   The threshold that retires Baγatur in the paper is 1 in 37, and
   the best branch of 冒頓 is 1 in 9. This is cheaper than either.
   The transcription and the morphology select the same form, and
   they were measured independently.

   WHAT IS NOT SETTLED, and must be said wherever this is used:
   - köngen is not itself an attested headword. It is a formation by
     a productive rule from an attested stem, which is a stronger
     position than a form requiring a lost consonant, but it is not
     an attestation.
   - The lexicons are eleventh century and the name is second century
     BCE. That -gAn was productive for Kāşgarî does not establish it
     1250 years earlier. This is the same gap the whole paper carries.
   - Whether -gAn formations were used as personal names or titles in
     Old Turkic is not tested here. Erdal's Grammar is the place.
   - The g-to-g rate is measured over all positions. Whether a g after
     a nasal behaves the same is not measured.
   - The first element is undetermined: kön, kün, kon and kun all cost
     the same. Choosing kön- is a semantic choice, which is the
     epithet habit S1.1 lists as a weakness.
