   THAT FIGURE IS NOT COMPARABLE WITH A WHOLE-NAME FIGURE. It prices
   one two-syllable element, and a short string is cheap by
   construction: every additional character multiplies in another
   rate below 1. Köngen's 1 in 5 at 軍臣 is a whole name, and 1 in 37
   retires Baγatur in §8.1. The comparison this figure supports is
   with the other element in the same slot, not with those.

   若鞮 is not priced here. The paper treats it in §9 as one title
   element shared by six names, and pricing it once per row would
   count the same evidence six times.

5.  呼都而尸道皋若鞮 priced, as far as it can be
======================================================================
   It cannot be priced, and that is the result. Two of its six
   non-title characters are asked to do something the corpus does 0
   times in 140, and a zero has no rate to multiply.
       呼 hɑ  → öz     vowel-initial on an h-   0 of 140
       皋 kou → uluğ   vowel-initial on a k-    0 of 140
   The remaining four characters carry nothing. Whatever the name
   says, this is not it.

7.  What this step establishes
======================================================================
   ESTABLISHES. Öz-Uluğ İnakt is not available. It needs a
   vowel-initial syllable written with a velar twice over, which is 0
   of 140, and it leaves four of eight characters carrying nothing,
   which the corpus does 0.00% of the time. It is the weakest reading
   in the file and should carry no proposal.

   ESTABLISHES. Uluğ at 烏累若鞮 is ordinary. The glottal is the
   majority spelling for a vowel-initial syllable, the liquid is
   written, and only the final -ğ is unwritten, which §8 measures at
   8 of 21.

   DOES NOT ESTABLISH. What the long name says. The frame searches
   above are offered as a starting point for someone else, not as a
   reading. Nothing in section 6 is proposed.

   A CAUTION ABOUT THE ZERO. The 0 of 140 is a Sanskrit figure. The
   claim it supports is about Chinese scribal practice, not about
   Turkic, and it is the same class of argument as §8.5's q and k
   measurement, with the same limitation stated there.
