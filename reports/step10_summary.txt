==========================================================================
 WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE XIONGNU MATERIAL
==========================================================================

  At 500 training pairs - roughly the volume of Han-era transcription
  data that exists - the Mongolian curve gives exact 7.1%, within-2 57.5%,
  mean normalised edit distance 0.334.

  At 7,530 pairs it gives exact 37.3%, within-2 88.4%, ned 0.142.

  Read the low end as the CEILING for the Xiongnu names under a perfect
  pipeline at the data volume available. Any claim about a specific Xiongnu
  reconstruction has to live inside that bound.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------
 THE BINDING VARIABLE IS OBSERVATIONS PER CHARACTER, NOT PAIRS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Compare the corpora on that axis:
    Mongolian, 500 pairs     :   9.5 observations per character
    Mongolian, full corpus   :  60.8
    Sanskrit, 858 pairs      :   5.0   <- sparser than the SMALLEST
                                        Mongolian sample, because Buddhist
                                        transcription uses a sprawling
                                        character inventory while the Secret
                                        History uses a tight conventionalised
                                        one
    Xiongnu names + titles   :   1.4   (112 character tokens over 81 distinct)

  That last row is the whole problem. At roughly one observation per
  character, a character-keyed channel cannot be estimated at all - there
  is no amount of clever search or better prior that recovers from it.

  CONSEQUENCE, and it is now empirically motivated rather than a guess:
  for Han-era material the channel MUST generalise across characters via
  their RECONSTRUCTED READING (Schuessler Later Han), not be keyed on the
  character itself. Characters sharing a reading then pool their evidence,
  and the ~90 Xiongnu characters collapse onto a far smaller set of
  reading types, each with many observations drawn from the whole Buddhist
  corpus. That is the only route that changes the arithmetic.

  Caveat: the Mongolian curve is same-language, same-period. Applying a
  channel across a 1,200-year phonological gap is strictly harder, so treat
  the low end as optimistic rather than as an achievable target.
