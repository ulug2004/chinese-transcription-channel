==========================================================================
 WHAT THIS MEANS FOR THE XIONGNU NAMES
==========================================================================

 Ming -> Later Han, by component:
   initial             79.3%
   nucleus             50.0%
   coda, nasal         99.2%
   coda, stop          57.8%   (majority guess only - the
                              three-way -p/-t/-k contrast is GONE)

 The asymmetry is sharp and it is not the one an aggregate figure shows.
 Nasal codas survive the 1,200-year jump intact. Stop codas are
 irrecoverable: Old Mandarin merged -p, -t and -k into zero, so given a
 Ming reading there is no evidence at all about which stop Later Han had.

 BUT - and this is why step 13 worked at all - the Ming transcription
 system COMPENSATED for that loss. Scribes marked foreign final consonants
 with an extra CHARACTER rather than a coda: 兒 (eul) for -r, 思 (sseu) for
 -s. Those were the two most frequent syllables in the Ligeti corpus
 (eul 118, sseu 61). So the information sits in the SYLLABLE SEQUENCE, not
 in the Chinese coda, and it is not lost by the period shift.

 CONSEQUENCE for the Xiongnu names - a precise boundary:
   * Initial place of articulation carries at ~79% across the gap.
     ONSET-level claims are supportable.
   * Nasal finals carry intact.
   * Stop finals do not carry at all. Any claim resting on a Xiongnu name
     having had a final -p, -t or -k cannot be supported from a Ming-trained
     channel, and Han-era transcription used codas directly rather than
     compensating characters, so that gap is real.
   * Nucleus at 50% is the weakest link and limits whole-form work
     independently of the coda question.

 CAVEATS
  1. n is 164 characters, and NOT a random sample - they are the nasal/stop-
     coda characters Tan tabulated. 72% carry a nasal coda, which is why the
     aggregate coda figure came out at a meaningless 87%. Split by class it
     is interpretable; do not quote a single coda number.
  2. Leave-one-out majority prediction is a weak learner. A real model would
     do better, so read these as lower bounds.
  3. Three different reconstruction traditions (Schuessler, Pulleyblank, Ning)
     with different notational conventions; some disagreement is notational
     rather than substantive.
