Step 49.  The ruling clan name: 攣鞮 and 虛連題.
Aligned one-character-to-one-syllable pairs: 772

======================================================================
1.  Why the two-character form resists reading
======================================================================
   A Chinese l- character writing a source syllable that begins with
   a vowel: 0 of 140.
       of those 140 syllables, ʔ      took 137  (98%)
       of those 140 syllables, ń      took   1  (1%)
       of those 140 syllables, t      took   1  (1%)
       of those 140 syllables, b      took   1  (1%)
   So el, which is vowel-initial, cannot be written by 攣.

   a source r written with a Chinese l-       177 of 200   88.5%
   a source l written with a Chinese l-        85 of 88    96.6%
   The source syllable began with a liquid. Old Turkic has no initial
   liquid, which is the wall. In 虛連題 the liquid is medial and
   the wall is not there, so that is the form searched below.

======================================================================
2.  NEW: how Turkic k and q were written in Chinese
======================================================================
   Counted on the Ligeti glossaries, which are Ming rather than
   Later Han. Used here because Sanskrit has no q at all, the same
   reason the paper uses this corpus for front rounded vowels.

   Turkic back q   n=66
       h-              43   65.2%
       aspirated k     20   30.3%
       plain k/g        2    3.0%
       other            1    1.5%

   Turkic front k  n=35
       aspirated k     31   88.6%
       h-               2    5.7%
       other            1    2.9%
       plain k/g        1    2.9%

   The two are written differently: a back q takes an h- character,
   a front k takes an aspirated one. That decides 虛, which is
   polyphonic with exactly those two values.

======================================================================
3.  What fits 虛連題
======================================================================
   Words matching  velar + V | liquid + V + nasal | dental + V  : 2
       karındaş       kardeş [DLT]
       kırındı        her şeyin kınntısı, kazıntısı, soyuntu su [DLT]

   karındaş, priced on each reading of 虛
       虛 = kʰɨɑ, a source plain k           0.425%   1 in 235
       虛 = hɨɑ,  a source back q           10.151%   1 in 9

   Nothing is inserted, nothing discarded, no metathesis: 虛 writes
   ka, 連 writes rın with its -n on the page, 題 writes daş with the
   sibilant unwritten, which Table 7 licenses at 75%.

   WHAT IS NOT SETTLED
   - The q rate is Ming, not Later Han. It cannot be checked against
     the Sanskrit corpus, which has no q.
   - The vowel of the second syllable is not separately measured; the
     open-vowel rate is used for it.
   - 攣鞮 is treated as an abbreviation of the longer form. The two
     are conventionally taken as variants of one name, but this step
     does not demonstrate the relation, it assumes it.
   - 虛 is absent from Schuessler's table under its traditional form
     and present under the simplified 虚. Four other characters of
     the record are in the same position: 戶 撑 祿 脫.
