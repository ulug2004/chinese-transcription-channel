----------------------------------------------------------------------
 CEILING IS LOW. The priors cannot reliably identify a language even when
 handed the actual word. The bottleneck is the PRIOR, not the channel.
 Root causes:
   - ASJP is a deliberately coarse 41-symbol alphabet; it discards exactly
     the detail that separates neighbouring families.
   - equalising to 469 forms per family (the Yeniseian floor) leaves very
     little data to fit a 3-gram on.
   - Swadesh entries are SHORT; a 5-segment word carries little phonotactic
     signal either way.
   - Turkic, Mongolic and Tungusic are phonotactically near-identical
     (vowel harmony, agglutinating, similar inventories and syllable shape).
     Generic n-grams cannot separate them from one short word, and no amount
     of channel improvement will fix that.

 WHAT WOULD FIX IT - targeted linguistic constraints instead of n-grams:
   * vowel harmony consistency  - strong in Turkic, absent in Yeniseian
   * initial-segment constraints - Old Turkic bans initial l-, r-, n-,
     and bans initial clusters; Yeniseian permits both
   * permitted codas and syllable shapes
   These are few-parameter features that work on short words, which is
   exactly the regime here. Generic n-grams are the wrong tool.

 Also consider narrowing the question to the live debate - Turkic vs
 Yeniseian - rather than six families at once. Yeniseian is the most
 phonotactically distinct of the set, and binary chance is 50%.
