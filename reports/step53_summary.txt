Step 53.  羯, and whether the transcription can separate two readings.
   Schuessler gives 羯 as ɨat.

======================================================================
1.  What a character with this vowel writes
======================================================================
   character tokens whose Schuessler vowel contains ɨa: 28
       source a     28  100.0%

   the corpus-wide distribution of source vowels, for comparison:
       source a   1187   53.8%
       source ā    322   14.6%
       source i    245   11.1%
       source u    232   10.5%
       source ī     90    4.1%
       source e     45    2.0%

   A source e is 2.0% of the corpus and 0 of 28 in this column.
   On the Later Han layer the character writes a source a.

======================================================================
2.  What that does to each reading
======================================================================
   *ket, the Yeniseian reading, has a front e. That vowel does not
   appear in the column above at all. This is the first item in the
   record where the transcription tells against a Yeniseian reading
   rather than licensing it alongside a Turkic one, as at 閼氏.

   kıyat, the reading in this supplement, has two syllables and 羯 is
   one character. The character's vowel is a diphthong, so a glide is
   available without inserting anything, but the word itself is the
   problem: see below.

   kıyat    is NOT a headword in any of the three lexicons
   kiyat    is NOT a headword in any of the three lexicons
   ket      is NOT a headword in any of the three lexicons
   kat      IS a headword: kat [DLT]
   kad      IS a headword: kar fırtınası, insan öldüren bora, tipl [DLT]

   Neither reading's word is attested. kat 'layer' and kad, which
   Kāşgarî glosses 'kar fırtınası, insan öldüren bora, tipi', a
   blizzard or a killing gale, are the attested words that fit, and
   neither is an obvious ethnonym.

======================================================================
4.  What this step does and does not establish
======================================================================
   ESTABLISHES. On the Later Han layer, 羯 writes a source a, 28 of
   28. A front e is not among them, and a source e is only 2.0% of
   the corpus, so the absence is not an artefact of rarity alone.

   DOES NOT ESTABLISH. That the Yeniseian reading is wrong. Two
   objections stand against using this measurement here at all:
     - The Jie appear in the fourth century CE. A Later Han value for
       a fourth-century ethnonym mixes periods, which §7 of the paper
       warns against in general terms. This is the first thing to
       settle, and this step does not settle it.
     - *keʔt is a PROTO-Yeniseian reconstruction. The vowel of the
       fourth-century word it descends from is not given by it, and
       Vovin proposes the comparison, not the vowel.

   AND IT CUTS THE WAY THE PAPER MUST BE MOST CAREFUL ABOUT. A result
   that weakens a Yeniseian reading and leaves a Turkic frame standing
   is the one kind of finding this paper should present with the
   objections attached, not the conclusion.
