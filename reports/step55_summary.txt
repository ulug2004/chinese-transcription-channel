How to read the two lists together
======================================================================
   呼都而: 4 fits.   尸道皋: 111 fits.
   Best of each, multiplied:
       kayıñ 0.010% x şebük 0.998% = 0.00010%, 1 in 1005115
   Against 1 in 37, which retires Baγatur in §8.1, and 1 in 5, the
   cheapest whole name in the supplement.

   THE TWO FRAMES FAIL IN OPPOSITE WAYS, AND BOTH FAILURES ARE REAL.
   呼都而 is empty for a structural reason, not for want of looking:
   而 has a central vowel, and a central Chinese vowel takes any
   source vowel only 4 to 6 percent of the time, so the third
   syllable costs about a factor of twenty whatever fills it; and
   而's ń- accepts a source ñ at 75%%, c at 12%% and y at 2%%, while a
   source n is 0. Four headwords in 9323 clear both, and the best of
   them is a birch tree at 1 in 10,000.

   尸道皋 fails the other way. It has room for 111 words, and its
   cheapest occupants are common nouns: quick, urine, cat, a lost
   thing, the lazy one. Nothing in it reads as a ruler's title.

   A CROWDED FRAME IS EVIDENCE AGAINST ITSELF. Section 6.3 of the
   paper measures that looking a reconstruction up in a Turkic
   dictionary finds a plausible word whether or not one is there. The
   count above is the size of that haystack, and a hit inside a large
   one carries no weight on its own.

======================================================================
What this step establishes
======================================================================
   ESTABLISHES. How much room each frame has, and what the cheapest
   occupants of it are. Nothing more.

   DOES NOT ESTABLISH. That the 3-3-2 cut is right. The Chinese marks
   no division, and step 54's finding stands: nothing about this row
   is proposed, and none of the words above is a candidate until the
   cut itself is argued from something other than the wish for one.

   DOES NOT ESTABLISH. That the Later Han layer is the right one. The
   name is from Hou Hanshu 89, a century later than the bulk of the
   record, and 尸 looks like an Eastern Han title element: 0 of 20 in
   the Western Han ruler names, 7 of 13 in the Southern Xiongnu
   titles, always as 尸逐. If it is formulaic, frame two is not a
   word frame at all and every figure in it is beside the point.
