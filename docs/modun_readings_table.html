冒頓: the three readings against the three candidates

Working note, 3 September 2026. Not part of the paper or the supplement.

The first character is not in dispute

冒 has two Later Han readings, mək and mouᶜ. Both Schuessler and Pulleyblank assign the first to this compound, and Pulleyblank names it: covet; Modun, founder of the Xiongnu empire, K1062a, EMC mak. Every candidate below therefore starts from mək, whose written -k is what allows one character to carry two source syllables.

Each reading admits exactly one or two candidates, and they are not the same ones. The reading printed in Appendix A is the only one of the three on which the author's own proposal survives. That does not make the assignment wrong: taking a character's primary reading is a defensible default, and it is the author's to make. But the coincidence is exactly what §6.3 measures, so the reason for preferring row 1 over the row Schuessler names for this name has to be stated in the paper rather than left implicit. On row 2, the one Schuessler assigns, Baγatur is better supported than Bögü Tuğ by roughly four to one and carries no historical objection.

What step 34 measured, and it is a rule worth having on its own

The question was whether a written coda has to match the place of articulation of the source coda. It does not, but it does have to match the manner.

Place is negotiable, manner is not. Of 58 stop-final source syllables whose coda was written, 41 kept the place and 17 changed it, 29%. But a source nasal was written as a stop 0 times in 159, and a source stop as a nasal once in 58. The 36% of stop-final syllables written with an open character reproduces Table 7's 30% on a different subset.

That asymmetry is what decides the matrix above. Every cell marked "fails" for Bögü Tın fails on manner, which never bends. The cell that changed for Bögü Tuğ changed because place does.

Costs every candidate pays equally, so they decide nothing between them

  • One character for two source syllables, with a written stop coda doing the compressing: 47 of the 1,017 verified pairs, 4.6%. Bögü Tın, Bögü Tuğ and Baγatur all require it, for bögü and baγa respectively.
  • A Chinese m- character for a source b-: 3 of 22 in the Later Han layer, 13.6% (§8.1), corrected from the period-mixed 0.4%.
  • The vowels are free. §7 puts vowel recovery at 50.0% with the interval straddling one half, so no candidate is preferred or refused on a vowel.

What is not measured yet

  • Only three Turkic words fit the tuət slot at all: tat "foreigner", tıt "larch", tut "rust". So on Schuessler's reading there is no Turkic compound and Baγatur stands alone. That search was ad hoc and needs a numbered run.
  • Step 23's 37 liquid opportunities remain the weakest sample in the table: every Baγatur cell rests on them.
