# 閼氏 - open question, 2 September 2026

**RESOLVED. This is now in the supplement and the header below is out of date.**
The 閼氏 entry reads *Elti* "lady" and carries all three measurements recorded
here: the 134 of 143 (93.7%) open-junction figure, the 2 of 143 (1.4%) written
`-t`, and the 38% at which a source liquid coda takes a `-t`. The polyphony
question at the end is answered in the entry from Baxter and Sagart: Schuessler
gives 閼 one value and Baxter-Sagart give Middle Chinese *ʔat* from Old Chinese
*\*qˤat*, so the written `-t` is real and the 1.4% problem does not go away by
that route; the `-n` belongs to 焉 in the parallel spelling 焉支, a different
character. The earlier *ece* / *eci* proposal is recorded inside the entry
rather than removed. Kept below as the working record of how it was settled.

## New measurements (Sanskrit corpus, 772 one-character-to-one-syllable pairs)

Not yet scripted as a numbered step. Both were run ad hoc; rerun before citing.

1. Source has a SINGLE affricate or sibilant between two vowels: 143 junctions.
   Preceding character coda is  open 134 (93.7%), -t 2 (1.4%), -k 5, -p 1, -ng 1.
   So a source *ece* predicts an open first character, not a written -t.

2. Chinese spells  -t coda + affricate/sibilant onset: 37 junctions.
   Source had a cluster 25 (68%), a single consonant 12 (32%).
   So the written -t behaves like the first half of a cluster.

Consequence: *ece* has to be one of the 1.4%. That is a worse result than the
1 of 37 (3%) used in section 8.1 to retire *Bagatur*. Consistency requires
either conceding the cost in the entry or finding a reading that uses the -t.

## Lexical check

Frame  vowel + t + {c, c-cedilla, j, s, s-cedilla, z} + vowel:
27 headwords in the DLT index, 6 in the Codex Cumanicus. None means consort,
queen, lady or wife. Closest: etci "butcher", etec / etic (a children's game
hole), atac (precocious child), otaci "physician". So *etce* fits the spelling
and is not a word.

Correction to an earlier claim of mine: *ece* IS in Kasgari. DLT index has
   ece  "buyuk kiz kardesh"   (I, 86)
   eci  "yashli kadin, hanim nine"  (I, 87)
*eci*, a respectful term for a senior woman, is a better semantic fit for a
principal consort than *ece* "elder sister". Clauson's ACA note was about which
forms are original versus Mongolian loans, not about absence from the Diwan.

## The question to settle first

Schuessler's LHantab gives 閼 exactly one value, ʔɑt. But the compound is
conventionally read *yanzhi*, and the parallel spelling 焉支 has 焉 = ʔɨan,
with -n. 閼 is polyphonic in Middle Chinese. If the reading here is the *yan*
one, the written -t does not exist and the 1.4% problem disappears.

Needs a source that lists 閼's readings: Schuessler's dictionary rather than the
table, or Baxter-Sagart. Neither is staged.

Also noted: 氏 has four values in LHantab (dźeᴮ, gieᴮ, tśe, kie), not one. The
supplement calls this item fully character-anchored, which is true of the
emission row but reads as more constraint than there is. Worth rewording
whatever the outcome.

## Prior proposals

Found none for *ece* at 閼氏. The literature has only the internal-Chinese claim
that 胭脂 "rouge" derives from 閼氏, plus Turkish work on the office of the
yanzhi with no etymology. Pulleyblank 1962 Part II appendix is the likely place
for a prior proposal and is still unobtained.
