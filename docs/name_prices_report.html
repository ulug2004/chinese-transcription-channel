The headline

Seventeen of the 28 priced rows are dearer than 1 in 37, which is the figure that retires Baγatur in §8.1. By the standard the paper already applies, more than half the supplement’s priced readings sit on the wrong side of its own threshold.

That is not automatically a verdict. A price is a ranking device, not a probability, and a longer name is dearer by construction. But it is the number a reviewer will compute for themselves, so we should be the ones to publish it.

Three things that need your decision

1. 若鞎 as İnakt comes out at 1 in 232 million

This is the paper’s own reading, argued in §9, and the only one in the supplement the paper puts its weight behind.

The cause: 若 is ń-, a palatal nasal, and a source n is written with it 0 times in 126.

But that zero is almost certainly an artefact, and I have built the distinction into the engine. Sanskrit has both a dental n and a palatal ñ, so a Sanskrit n always had a Chinese n- available and never needed a palatal character. The corpus therefore never tested the substitution. Turkic has no palatal nasal at all, so a Turkic n facing a ń- character is a situation Sanskrit could not produce. The engine now labels these inventory-gap zeros and keeps them separate from contested zeros such as the 0 of 140.

So §9 is not refuted. But the paper currently rests an argument on a correspondence its own corpus cannot price, and does not say so. That needs a sentence, and I would not draft it without you.

2. The two dearest real proposals

且鞎侯 Çatıgka at 1 in 224,965 and 屠耳 Doğru at 1 in 188,583. Both are three or four orders of magnitude beyond anything else in the file. Çatıgka’s own entry already admits it “adds material the transcription does not have”. Doğru has no such admission and no figure in its entry.

Two errors of mine, one of which I told you yesterday

I mapped the Turkic velar nasal to the wrong Sanskrit phoneme in step 55. Turkic ñ / ng is a velar nasal, and I priced it against Sanskrit ñ, which is palatal. It should be Sanskrit ṅ.

This changes what I told you about kayıñ and kayın. I said the frame at 而 admitted the birch tree at 75% and excluded the kinsman at 0 of 126, and made something of the transcription choosing the useless word. Under the correct mapping both are excluded: a Turkic velar nasal on ń- is 0 of 7, and a Turkic n on ń- is 0 of 126. The frame is closed to both, and my nice story about the tree and the kinsman was an artefact of my own mapping error. The conclusion about that row is unchanged and in fact stronger, but the reasoning I gave you was wrong and you should not repeat it.

Four characters were silently unpriceable. 戶 虛 禄 脱 are tabulated by Schuessler only in their simplified shapes. The engine now maps the traditional form onto the simplified one and records the substitution, which is what made 當戶 Tamga and 虛闷權漁 Kalkan priceable at all. 撒 is absent in both shapes and remains a genuine hole.

What I did not do

Nothing was rewritten, retired, or restatused, no document was rebuilt, and nothing was pushed. The corpus fix is written to nti_transcription_pairs_v2.csv alongside the original, with a provenance column on every row, so it can be regenerated either way and adopted only when you say so.
